# Competitors and Opportunities

Competitor selection, the opportunity register and its routes are operating methods. Google statements carry source IDs.

## Choose competitors for a stated question

Record why each comparison is relevant. A tool's list of competing domains is not the market.

| Set | Selection basis | Use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business alternatives | Same customer task, a realistic substitute and the same market | Choices, positioning and commercial constraints. They may not be the strongest search competitors |
| Search competitors | Recurring relevant results for representative tasks, in a dated observation context | Page-type fit, task coverage and the evidence pages offer. Visibility does not prove business performance |
| Local competitors | Comparable real-world service and location context, with local result observations | Local relevance and customer choice. Record where the observation was made, because distance matters |
| Editorial or information sources | Pages serving a research or decision task in scope | Useful explanations or missing evidence. Not automatically competitors for the sale |
| AI feature sources | A named platform and feature, prompt or task, market and the sources observed | Route to an AI-visibility check. A sampled citation is not market share |

Use a small set that represents the task and widen it only when results are mixed. There is no fixed number. Record exact URLs, the date, market, language, device and method where relevant. If no competitor pages were inspected, write `Not assessed`.

Assess what each competing page helps a visitor do, the evidence it offers and the real offer behind it, then ask what this business can usefully provide. Do not copy structure, claims, headings, word counts, reviews or markup because another site ranks.

Third-party tools do not have access to Google's internal ranking data and cannot guarantee performance [S08]. Label estimated traffic, difficulty and authority metrics with their provider. They do not establish Google's judgement, revenue or a ranking cause.

## Opportunity register

Record a problem or audience opportunity before proposing a deliverable. For each candidate capture:

- ID, the affected offer or task, audience and market, and the exact page, section or profile if known.
- The observed trigger with its source and date, or a hypothesis marked as awaiting validation.
- Business relevance and whether the business can realistically meet the need.
- The existing asset and the smallest plausible action, with alternatives including no action.
- The type of benefit expected, with no invented uplift figure.
- Evidence confidence, any unresolved cause and the next check that would separate the explanations.
- Effort band, maintenance burden, dependencies, owner and permission state.
- Policy, technical or commercial risk, the acceptance check, the outcome measure and the review trigger.

## Routes

| Route | Evidence that could justify investigation | Action or handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access, indexing or discovery | A confirmed defect on an important page, with its scope and the observed output | Technical diagnosis and repair. Do not assume every tool flag affects rankings |
| Existing-page usefulness | A supported mismatch with intent or the offer, missing decision help, factual gaps or a broken next step | Improve the existing page through page planning and the content evidence and briefing workflow |
| Missing customer task | A research-supported task that fits the business, no suitable existing page and a feasible contribution | Keyword research, then page planning. A new URL stays unapproved until it passes those checks |
| Architecture and internal links | Important pages are hard to discover or task routes are unclear | Page and link planning, verified on the live site |
| Local and Business Profile | An eligible business context plus a verified information mismatch or local need | A location-level local audit. Do not create profiles or town pages to widen a coverage map |
| Ecommerce and merchant | Real inventory, a shopping task, or a crawl or data-feed question | The relevant technical, structured data and merchant guidance. Do not index every facet |
| Structured data | Visible verified facts, current markup output and a supported need | An evidence-led keep, repair, add, remove or defer decision |
| AI features | An applicable feature, an ordinary search eligibility question or reproducible visibility evidence | An AI-visibility check. No special AI writing style, special markup or page per fan-out question |
| Reputation and links | Genuine business evidence, a broken important reference, a useful resource or a specific unnatural-link concern | The reputation rules below. Technical or policy incidents go to diagnosis |
| Measurement and customer journey | An important outcome cannot be measured, or two sources disagree about it | Investigate the definitions, counting, coverage and processing before declaring success or failure. Change configuration only after a confirmed finding |

Google says its ranking systems are designed to work at page level, that site-wide signals and classifiers are also used, and that good or poor site-wide signals do not mean all of a site's content will rank the same way [S12]. Do not prioritise a whole-site rewrite because a few pages declined.

For structured data, Google's AI features guide says there is no special markup needed for generative AI search and no new machine-readable or AI text files are required to appear in Google Search [S13]. Do not add an AI workstream just because a feature exists.

## Reputation, authority and links

Start from the real business: accurate identity, relevant expertise and credentials, transparent terms, customer service, genuine reviews, useful original resources and legitimate recognition. These can help customers and discovery. They are not a universal set of ranking inputs or a substitute for a good offer.

| Situation | Evaluate | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing or unclear credibility evidence | What a customer needs to verify and what can be substantiated | Never invent credentials, authors, experience, awards or results |
| A broken important external reference | The live source and destination, its purpose and a suitable replacement | Verify the link. A reclamation idea does not authorise contacting anyone |
| Local identity or citation mismatch | The correct details and who owns each listing | Local audit. No bulk low-value directory work or invented locations |
| Useful resource or partnership | Audience benefit, real expertise, publisher fit, effort and permission | Only within authorised scope, with no placement or ranking promise |
| Suspicious backlink report | The actual links and history, and any manual action or known link activity | Diagnosis. Do not prescribe a disavow from a vendor toxicity score alone |

Google's spam policies prohibit creating links mainly to manipulate rankings, including buying or selling links for ranking and excessive link exchanges [S10]. Never turn an authority gap into paid ranking links, manipulative exchanges, fake reviews or inauthentic mentions. Outreach is conditional work, not a default deliverable, and this skill never sends messages or contacts anyone.
