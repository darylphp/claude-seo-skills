# Scope and Intake

Everything in this file is an operating method unless a source ID is shown.

## Strategy situations

Pick the situation that fits the request. The output column is what the pack should deliver for that situation.

| Situation | Starting scope | Useful output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New account or inherited strategy | Business facts, access, earlier decisions and a dated baseline | Initial priorities, uncertainties and research boundaries |
| Established business with a new offer or market | What changed and which existing assets could serve it | A bounded opportunity assessment and revised exclusions |
| Traffic or lead decline | Confirm the metric, period and affected scope first | A diagnosis request and provisional priorities, not an assumed cause. Usually **Diagnose before prioritising** |
| Limited communication or permissions | Current approved facts and the evidence that is available | Work that can proceed, blocked decisions and one useful request |
| New website or major redevelopment | Business tasks, current pages and assets where they exist, measurement and release constraints | Strategic requirements and a handoff for migration or design specialists |
| A new AI, schema or local feature is proposed | Eligibility, business relevance, evidence and ongoing cost | Assess, pilot, monitor, defer or no action |

Write the strategy question as one paragraph before choosing tools or sources. Do not assume the agency's or user's location is the business's target market.

A routine, already authorised repair does not need a full strategy exercise. Reuse approved information and earlier work instead of onboarding the business again.

## Minimum business record

Use what has been supplied first. Ask only for the missing detail that would change the decision. Relative priorities, such as "focus on these two services", are enough when confidential financial figures are not needed.

| Input | Minimum useful answer | Why it changes the strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and business model | Trading name, website, service, product or publisher model and the responsible contact | Prevents work for the wrong entity or commercial model |
| Offer priorities | What is actually sold or provided, priority offers, exclusions and available capacity or stock | Stops demand research turning into a plan for work the business cannot do |
| Audience and task | Who needs it, their problem, decision criteria, objections and the next step wanted | Defines useful research and page tasks |
| Market | Real service or delivery coverage, countries or regions, language and commercial differences | Controls localisation, competitor choice and eligibility |
| Business outcome | Qualified enquiries, bookings, sales or another defined outcome | Separates valuable work from traffic or publishing targets |
| Delivery constraints | Budget or hours band, CMS, implementer, access, approval process and maintenance owner | Makes the priorities executable |
| Evidence and differentiation | Real experience, assets, experts, methods, products, customer questions and permission to use them | Tests whether useful content can be produced accurately |
| Existing work and history | Earlier priorities, releases, migrations, known incidents, important pages and current commitments | Avoids duplicate tasks and false explanations of cause |
| Success and review | What improvement would matter, available measurement and a decision or review date | Defines how priorities will be reassessed |

Do not invent margins, customer demographics, sales cycles, credentials, branches, product capabilities or profitable services. Record commercial assumptions separately from approved facts.

## When to ask and when to return

- Ask at most three focused questions per attempt, and only questions whose answers would change the strategy.
- Missing paid-tool access, exact margins or a full analytics history is not a blocker by itself. Continue with what is available and label the limit.
- An unknown business identity, offer, market or outcome can invalidate the work. If the answers are not available, return **Return for business facts**, keep any confirmed facts, and name exactly what would let the work start.
- Do not treat silence after a number of days as approval.

## Urgent routes

A confirmed broken enquiry or booking path, an outage or a security incident goes straight to repair or diagnosis with whoever is authorised to act. Record it as the first opportunity with its acceptance check. Strategy work must not postpone an authorised containment or a clear repair.

For a traffic or lead decline with no confirmed cause, confirm the metric, period and affected scope, list the competing explanations and route the investigation to technical diagnosis. Give provisional priorities only for work that does not depend on the cause. Google lists several possible explanations for a drop, including technical, security and spam issues, algorithmic updates, seasonality and site moves [S14].
