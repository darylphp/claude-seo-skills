**Deferred or rejected, with reasons**

- **Suburb pages (O6).** Location pages are not banned, but each needs a justified customer task, accurate service coverage and a page-specific contribution. As described, the set includes places the business does not serve, which this pack rejects on business grounds and because those enquiries would not qualify. For suburbs inside coverage, Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse and scaled content abuse [S10], and creating separate content for every query variation mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses is scaled content abuse [S13]. The proposed pages' content is not described, so this is a risk to assess at page planning, not a finding that the proposal breaks policy. More listed places is not evidence of ranking across them: Google's local help names relevance, distance and prominence as the main local factors and says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking [S06].
- **More blogs (O7).** Google's people-first guidance lists warning signs such as producing lots of content on many topics in the hope some performs and writing to a word count [S09]. The evidence points to a service page gap, not a publishing gap.
- **Schema (O8).** As an operating method, this pack treats structured data as a description of visible, verified facts and a route to documented feature eligibility, not as a ranking lever. Google says no special markup is needed for its generative AI search features [S13]. Nothing observed calls for markup work now.
- **Kitchen growth (O9).** Retained without change while kitchens are booked about 10 weeks ahead.
- **Site-wide rewrite.** Not recommended. Google says its ranking systems work mainly at page level and that site-wide signals do not mean all content ranks the same way [S12]. The gap found is specific to `/wardrobes/`.

**Timing.** The summer wardrobe goal makes O1 and O2 time-sensitive, but Google does not guarantee indexing and says changes can take from a few hours to several months to be reflected [S11]. Supported interpretation: an improved page also helps visitors who already reach it from the home page, the profile or referrals, so its value does not rest only on a ranking change.

**No forecast.** Missing inputs: wardrobe search demand inside the install area (E1 impressions include searchers outside it and omit anonymised queries), any sourced basis for a visibility change, the repaired form's visit-to-request rate, the wardrobe qualification rate (E3 does not split qualified requests by offer) and, optionally, typical wardrobe job value. The form fault also interrupts the baseline.
