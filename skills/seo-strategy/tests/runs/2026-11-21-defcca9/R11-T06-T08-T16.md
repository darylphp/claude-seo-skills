## 7. Priorities and trade-offs

- **O1: Address a confirmed failure.** The quote path for the priority offer failed a test, and the log shows no wardrobe form requests since 21 Oct, so containment and repair come before anything else.
- **O2: Improve a supported opportunity.** Business fit, customer questions, a suitable existing page and permitted assets are all in hand, so a scoped page improvement with an acceptance check is justified.
- **O3: Validate before committing.** The event and log gap must be explained before Analytics events are used to judge O2 or any tracking is changed.
- **O4: Validate before committing.** A local audit of the existing profile is cheap, but nothing about the profile has been inspected yet.
- **O5: Retain, defer or reject (deferred).** Spare capacity is real but no laundry demand evidence was supplied.
- **O6: Retain, defer or reject (rejected outside coverage, deferred inside).** Pages for places the business does not serve would misstate coverage; any in-coverage page needs its own page-specific task first.
- **O7: Retain, defer or reject (deferred).** The questions buyers ask fit the existing service page better than new posts.
- **O8: Retain, defer or reject (deferred).** No markup has been inspected and no supported need exists.
- **O9: Retain, defer or reject (rejected as market definition).** National retailers are not the customer's alternative for custom installed wardrobes in Hobart.
- **O10: Retain, defer or reject (no action).** Kitchens are capacity-limited, not visibility-limited.

**Delayed because of capacity.** With about 6 contractor hours a month, O1 and then O2 take the available hours. The contractor's part of O3 should be done alongside the O1 diagnosis where possible, because it involves the same form and event. O4 needs mainly the owner's time and can run in parallel if the owner has it. O5, O7 and O8 wait until O2 is live and verified. The owner's summer goal makes O1 and O2 time-sensitive, but this pack sets no dates: the contractor's hours and the owner's approvals set the pace.

**Policy notes on the proposal.** Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse as pages made to rank for similar queries that lead users to less useful intermediate pages, and scaled content abuse as many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings [S10]. Whether in-coverage area pages would fit depends on what each would contain, which the proposal does not say, so no violation is claimed. Pages for Launceston, the north-west or anywhere beyond about 40 minutes fail on accuracy before policy is considered. Google's local guidance names relevance, distance and prominence as the main local factors and says a better local ranking cannot be requested or paid for [S06], so a longer list of places on the site is not evidence of ranking across that area. Google's people-first guidance warns against producing lots of content on many topics in the hope some performs [S09], which applies to blog volume as a goal. Google's AI features guide says no special markup is needed for generative AI search [S13], so AI visibility is not a reason to add schema either.

**Declined requests from the owner, with reasons.**

- *Use Tool X's authority score and competitor list as the market:* declined (O9). The list is national furniture and homewares retailers, not businesses serving the same task in the same area, and Tool X's figures are estimates without Google's internal data [S08]. E7 is the better starting point for the wardrobe question.
- *Treat phrases with no Search Console rows, such as "built in wardrobe cost tasmania", as zero demand:* declined. Search Console omits anonymised queries and keeps only the most important rows [S02], and E1 was limited to the top 1,000 rows per table. These phrases are recorded as `Not available` in E1, not zero. The price question is already evidenced by nine callers asking about wardrobe price ranges (E6), so the decision does not depend on that phrase's volume. The phrase also names all of Tasmania, which is wider than the coverage area.
- *Combine Search Console clicks with Analytics sessions into one traffic number:* declined. Clicks measure search performance and sessions measure site interaction; they are different measures and will not match [S03]. Adding them would count visits from Google twice, and the two use different time bases (Search Console defaults to Pacific Time; E2 uses Australia/Hobart) [S03]. Sessions are also `Not available` in the supplied Analytics export. Report Search Console clicks and impressions, Analytics key events and the owner's logged and qualified requests side by side, each labelled with its source, period and timezone.

**No forecast.** Missing inputs: monthly wardrobe search demand within the coverage area (E1 is a 16-month aggregate with row limits); any observed basis for a click change after the page improvement; a reliable enquiry rate (sessions not supplied and events unreconciled); the qualification rate for wardrobe requests; job value; and the length of the form fault period.
