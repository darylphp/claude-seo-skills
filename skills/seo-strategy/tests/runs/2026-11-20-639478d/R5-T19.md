- E2 groups events by landing page and E3 by the owner's recorded source, so the two do not split requests the same way. This definitional difference is **confirmed**; its effect on the gap is untested.
- The event may fire on a button click or page change, not only on a processed submission.

- Spam or test submissions may count as events but are not logged as requests.
- Submissions may be stored by the plugin while their notifications are not delivered. E4 fits this for wardrobes but does not confirm it.
- The owner may log some web enquiries under another source, for example a form request that was then handled by phone.
- The two sources may use different timezones, and the edges of the period may not match.
- Consent or filtering differences may affect what Analytics records.

**Bounded scenario: `No forecast`.**

To answer the owner's question directly: no. With the inputs supplied, a bounded scenario cannot support spending contractor hours on `/wardrobes/`, and it cannot rule the spending out, so this pack does not build one. The case for O3 does not depend on a forecast. It rests on the observations in section 6 and on a small, reversible scope with an acceptance check and an outcome review.

Why a scenario would not meet the rules here:

- The most important input is how many more qualified wardrobe requests the improved page would bring. Nothing on this site or in the supplied evidence gives an observed basis for it, so any figure or range would be invented.
- There is no clean current baseline. The wardrobe form path appears to have failed at some point on or after 21 Oct, and events and logged requests disagree on every form.
- E8 shows 11 jobs from 40 wardrobe quotes in 2025. Those quotes came from unrecorded sources, are quotes issued rather than requests, and do not overlap the 2026 log, so the ratio cannot turn website requests into jobs.
- E8's A$2,800 to A$9,500 is job value, not margin.
- Neither the hours O3 would take nor the contractor's hourly cost has been supplied.
- The number of extra wardrobe jobs summer capacity allows, and the dates of the summer window, have not been supplied.
- Monthly wardrobe search data has not been supplied, so seasonality cannot be modelled.

Missing inputs: (1) the observed effect of a comparable page change; (2) a baseline of wardrobe requests taken while the form works; (3) quote sources and the ratio of requests to quotes; (4) hours for O3 and the contractor's hourly cost; (5) margin per wardrobe job (optional and confidential); (6) extra wardrobe jobs summer capacity allows; (7) monthly wardrobe search data from E1.

A threshold the owner can check privately (this is not a forecast): O3 contractor hours × hourly cost, plus the owner's time if the owner wants to count it, ÷ typical margin per wardrobe job = the number of extra won wardrobe jobs needed to cover the cost. It shows how much the change would need to produce, not whether it will.

What would make a scenario possible at the next review: the O2 log columns, a baseline with the form working, the actual hours O3 took, and a summer of recorded outcomes. Even then, a before-and-after difference would be an observation affected by the O1 repair and the season, not a measured effect of the page.
