**Location pages assessed against the actual proposal.** Google describes doorway abuse as pages made to rank for similar queries that lead users to less useful intermediate pages, and scaled content abuse as many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings rather than help users [S10]. Making separate content for every variation of a query, mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses, falls under scaled content abuse [S13]. Five pages for served suburbs is not a violation because of its count or its name, and it removes the main flaw of the earlier 30-page idea, which would have reached areas the business does not serve. The remaining risk cannot be judged until the contractor says what each page would contain. It would rise if the pages repeated the wardrobe page's text with the suburb name swapped and passed visitors on to `/wardrobes/` or `/contact/`. It would fall if each page gave visitors something they cannot get from the general page, such as Kingston new-build handover timing, New Norfolk older-home fitting or Sorell travel charges, backed by permitted local job photos. Glenorchy and Lindisfarne currently have job photos but no distinct customer questions noted. Several of the suburb questions may suit the general wardrobe page better, because uneven walls, builders' handover dates and travel charges are not unique to one suburb. A longer list of locations on the site is not evidence that the business will rank across that area: Google names relevance, distance and prominence as the main local factors and says a better local ranking cannot be requested or paid for [S06].

**What is delayed by capacity.** With about 6 contractor hours a month, O1 and then O3 take the contractor's time first. O5 waits until O3 is live and the location check has reached a decision. Any question-led page from O6 waits behind both. O2 and O4 need little contractor time and can run alongside. If O1 needs substantial work, the O3 build moves back, but the owner's preparation for O3 (price publication decision, lead times, photo selection and permission checks) can go ahead in parallel.

**Summer timing.** A repaired form helps current visitors as soon as it is verified. Page changes can take anywhere from a few hours to several months to be reflected in Search [S11], so this pack does not promise that O3 will bring wardrobe jobs before or during summer.

**Deferred or rejected:** O5 deferred pending validation; O6 blog cadence rejected and question-led pages deferred; O7 retained with no demand work; O8 deferred; structured data and AI work not proposed, because no need was found and no special markup or AI files are needed [S13]; the 30 Tasmania-wide suburb pages stay excluded.

**No forecast.** Missing inputs: qualified rate by offer and source, the relationship between recorded events and real submissions (O2), wardrobe job values and win rate, suburb-qualified demand, and any post-change data.
