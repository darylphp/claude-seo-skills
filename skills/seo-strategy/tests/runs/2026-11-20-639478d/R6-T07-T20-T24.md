**Delayed because of limited capacity.** The contractor has about 6 hours a month. O1, plus the O2 inspection of the same form system, comes first. If diagnosis uses most of those hours, the O3 build waits. Owner-side work for O3 can carry on meanwhile: choosing photos, deciding on price ranges, and writing the process and lead-time facts. O4 is owner-led and uses no contractor hours. O5 waits until O3 is planned, because the owner's summer priority is wardrobes. O9 waits until after O3, because it has no trigger.

**Summer timing.** This pack gives no timeline for search changes. Google says changes can take from a few hours to several months to be reflected, and that it does not guarantee indexing [S11]. Of everything here, the O1 repair is most directly tied to wardrobe enquiries this summer, because it affects visitors who already reach the page.

**Deferred or rejected items**

- **30 suburb pages across Tasmania (O7): rejected as proposed.** The proposal does not say what the pages would contain. Pages for Launceston, the north-west or anywhere else outside the roughly 40-minute coverage would misstate where the business works. They would also attract requests the owner marks as unqualified, and E6 already shows Launceston callers. Suppose each page repeated the same service copy with a suburb name swapped in and sent visitors to the same quote form. That would match the pattern Google's spam policies describe as doorway abuse: pages made to rank for similar queries that lead users to less useful intermediate pages [S10]. Google also says that creating separate content for every variation of a query, mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses, is scaled content abuse [S13]. This is a risk assessed against the likely shape of the proposal, not a finding about pages that do not exist. A page for a suburb inside coverage is not banned. It would need its own customer task and page-specific evidence, such as permitted jobs in that area, and none was supplied. On the "rank higher" premise: Google's local ranking help names relevance, distance and prominence as the main local factors and says no one can request or pay for a better local ranking [S06]. A longer list of place names on the site is not evidence of ranking across those places.
- **More blogs (O8): deferred.** Google's people-first guidance warns against producing lots of content on many topics in the hope that some performs [S09]. The customer questions in E6 come up at the point of choosing a wardrobe maker, so they belong on `/wardrobes/` first. Do not commit to a publishing cadence that 6 hours a month cannot sustain.
- **Schema (O9): deferred.** No markup problem or need was observed. Nothing in the evidence links markup to the wardrobe gap.
- **Kitchen growth (O6): retained without new work.** Kitchens are booked about 10 weeks ahead.
- **Reporting key events as qualified leads: rejected.** See section 1 and O2.
- **Changing the key event trigger: deferred** until O2 confirms a finding.
- **Rolling back the plugin: not recommended now.** It stays a conditional recovery route within O1.

**No forecast.** The pack makes no traffic, ranking, enquiry or revenue projection. A wardrobe scenario would need: a reliable post-repair baseline of wardrobe form requests; qualified rate by source and offer; wardrobe landing sessions; a monthly seasonal split of wardrobe queries; and an observed response to the O3 release. None of these is available yet.
