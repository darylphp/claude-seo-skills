# Families and Intent

## 1. Family test

This is an operating method, not Google's internal classification. Group queries when the reader's situation, task, desired outcome, next action, expected evidence and broadly compatible result types align. Similar words alone are not enough, and different words can describe the same task.

Tool clusters, embedding similarity and result-overlap scores can help triage. Record the method and settings, and review the decision yourself. A grouping score or family name is not Google's intent label.

| Usually group when the meaning aligns | Investigate a boundary when |
| --- | --- |
| Spelling, singular and plural, or reordered wording | Location or urgency changes the actual service or task |
| Close synonyms for the same outcome | Browsing a class differs from evaluating one item |
| Short and question forms of the same need | Learning differs from booking, implementing or resolving |
| Modifiers that restate a shared task | Audience, constraints, comparison set or proof differs |

Give each family a stable ID and keep its row references. Keep the IDs through later revisions and explain any split, merge or change of interpretation.

## 2. Intent profile

Record for each family:

- the reader, their situation and the job to be done
- the main outcome and the likely next action
- the decision stage: discover, understand, evaluate, select, act, use or resolve
- the dominant and any secondary intent, using informational, commercial, transactional or navigational only as shorthand
- local, brand, urgency, risk and other constraints
- the information, format, interaction and evidence the reader expects
- supporting and contradicting result observations, and open questions

Keep brand and navigational queries in their own family. They show existing awareness, not a new opportunity.

## 3. Mixed and ambiguous intent

When one query serves competing needs, keep them visible. Do not force an ambiguous head term into one family because a tool gave it a single label. Record the competing interpretations, the evidence for each, a temporary treatment and the next check. Whether those needs suit one page, several pages or a section of a page is a planning decision, not a research one.

## 4. Boundary example

Hypothetical strings only. No volumes, results or client facts are implied.

| Candidate wording | Initial treatment | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "heat pump installation cost" and "heat pump installation price" | Possibly one cost task | Reader context, observed results and meaningful market differences |
| "heat pump installation" and "heat pump installation requirements" | Related but possibly different tasks | Booking intent compared with feasibility or technical evidence |
| "heat pump installer [town]" | A local candidate, within real coverage only | Proximity, availability and whether evidence supports that market |

The example shows what needs checking. It is not a URL plan.
