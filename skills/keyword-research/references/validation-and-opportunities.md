# Validation and Opportunities

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. Result observations

Record the exact query, engine and surface, date, country and location setting, language, device, collection method and any personalisation context known. Say when the method cannot reproduce a precise local context. A generic search is not a verified town-level mobile result.

Inspect the relevant pages themselves when the classification depends on their purpose, because titles and snippets can mislead. Separate ads, local results, organic links and AI features. One snapshot does not prove a lasting pattern.

| Observe | Interpret cautiously |
| --- | --- |
| Service, product, category, guide, comparison, forum or tool results | Possible reader expectations, not a required template |
| Local, shopping, video, image, news or AI features | Context and competing attention, not a click forecast |
| Audience, offer, evidence and task on the relevant pages | Fit and expectations. Do not copy their claims. |
| Differences between variants | Evidence for a family boundary. A shared domain alone is weak evidence. |
| Changes across dates or markets | Volatility or context that needs a qualification |

**Google guidance [K18]:** AI Overviews and AI Mode may use query fan-out, running related searches across subtopics. Outside researchers cannot see the fan-out queries for a particular search. Model-generated questions are candidates, not observed prompts or fan-out logs.

## 2. Sampling and stopping

As an operating minimum, check at least one representative query for each material family, and more variants when ambiguity, market, risk or result type could change the decision. Go deeper for costly page or architecture decisions. This minimum is not statistical proof or a Google requirement.

If you cannot observe results in the right market, return a validation plan and keep the family provisional. Never write "results checked" or "Google prefers this page type" without the observations. Keep failed, unavailable and no-result checks separate.

Stop when the evidence supports the handoff or shows a specific open question. Do not keep expanding to grow the row count.

## 3. Specific-task and long-tail candidates

Do not define an opportunity by phrase length. Record how specific the query is and what frequency evidence exists, separately. Evaluate each candidate on:

1. the real audience need and its evidence status
2. fit with an actual offer, audience and serviceable market
3. what the reader must understand, decide or do
4. existing research and any observed URL signal
5. the proof, utility and maintenance a responsible answer needs
6. the demand evidence available and its limits
7. ambiguity or conditions that could change the interpretation

| Evidence pattern | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated genuine customer question, no tool volume | Keep as a supported task. Volume stays `Not available`. |
| Tool reports zero, business relevance is credible | Keep the reported value and its source. Investigate rather than discard. |
| Large estimate, unrelated to the offer | Exclude or restrict, with the reason |
| One task expressed through many modifiers | Group the variants. No automatic separate page. |
| A model suggests a plausible niche problem without a source | Inferred candidate. Say how to validate it. |
| Safety, compatibility, legal or financial specifics | Needs verified facts from the right source. Do not supply an answer. |

Never promise easier rankings, higher conversion or predictable traffic because a phrase is long or rare.

## 4. Entities and decision criteria

Start from the reader's decision, then identify the subjects, attributes, relationships, standards, dependencies, constraints and alternatives that matter. Use approved client facts and primary sources for material claims.

| Class | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Required | Needed to answer the task accurately |
| Useful | Adds meaningful context or comparison |
| Conditional | Relevant only to a named audience, product, location or scenario |
| Exclude | Irrelevant, unsupported, misleading or out of scope |

Record why each item matters and what evidence would support it. Do not turn the map into density targets, mandatory headings, schema, an LSI keyword list or a topical authority score.

**Google guidance [K10]:** Google's systems aim to reward helpful, reliable content made for people. Semantic research here improves accuracy and completeness. It is not a way to decorate copy with terms.

## 5. Competitor gaps

Separate business competitors from sites that appear for the sampled query. For each candidate gap, record the query or task, the competitor page actually inspected, what it contributes, the site's observed coverage, business fit and the proof needed. "A competitor has this page" is not enough, and missing wording is not necessarily a missing task. Return gaps as candidates for planning, with no slugs, redirects or priorities.

## 6. Niche lenses

Apply only the lens that fits.

| Context | Distinctions to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Local service | Real coverage, urgency, service scope, trust evidence and contact intent |
| Ecommerce | Category browsing compared with one product or variant, compatibility, availability, delivery and returns |
| SaaS and B2B | Buyer and practitioner roles, integration, implementation, security and purchase stage |
| Reviews and comparisons | Fair criteria, alternatives, testing status and disclosure. No invented experience. |
| Expert or high-risk topics | Evidence standards, expert input, uncertainty and consequences |
| International | Real market, language and offer differences, not mechanical translation |

**Google guidance [K09]:** doorway abuse and scaled content abuse are spam. A long candidate list is not permission to publish many similar pages.
