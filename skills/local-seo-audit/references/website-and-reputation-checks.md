# Website and Reputation Checks

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. Reviews and replies

**Google guidance [G11, G12, G13]:** reviews must reflect genuine experiences. A business must not offer incentives for reviews or for changing or removing them, selectively ask satisfied customers, discourage negative reviews, pressure customers on the premises, ask for specific content or set staff review quotas. A neutral request link or QR code is fine. Replies should be relevant and professional and protect privacy. Report a review only for a policy violation, not because the business dislikes it.

Check:

- How reviews are requested: channel, wording, who is asked, and whether any reward, rating request, filter or gate is involved.
- Who is authorised to reply, and whether that approval is recorded [G04].
- Reply quality and privacy in a sample. Replies should not confirm sensitive customer relationships.
- Suspicious reviews: keep the URL, the date and the specific policy reason. Do not promise removal or suggest retaliation.

Recommend one neutral request process for all genuine customers, with wording the business approves. Never recommend review targets, rating gates, incentives, disclosed or not, or writing reviews for customers. A recurring complaint is an operations finding as well as a reputation one.

## 2. Profile destination

Check the exact URL the profile links to: redirects, final status, intended indexing and canonical, mobile usability, whether the page matches this location's offer and contact route, and whether tracking parameters survive the redirect. Prefer the homepage or a genuine location page, whichever better lets the visitor complete the task. Hand wider technical problems to a technical SEO diagnosis.

## 3. Local and service-area pages

**Google guidance [G14]:** doorway abuse includes pages for specific regions or cities that funnel visitors to one page, and substantially similar pages that sit closer to search results than a browsable hierarchy. Blocks of text listing cities a page is trying to rank for are keyword stuffing. Local pages are not abusive by default.

For each existing or proposed location or service-area page, check:

- the distinct customer task and the real coverage for that area
- whether an existing URL already meets the need
- original local information the business can support, such as access details, service limits, genuine projects or staff input
- a working enquiry, booking or visit route
- who will maintain it

Recommend the smallest action the evidence supports: keep, improve, consolidate, redirect, defer or no action. A page for an area the business does not serve is an accuracy problem as well as a quality one. A service-area page can describe genuine coverage without claiming an office. Never recommend inventing a branch, address, team, testimonial or case study. Hand query research and new-page decisions to a keyword research or page-opportunity workflow.

## 4. Citations and structured data

Check external listings only where information is materially wrong, out of date or misleading, starting with the ones customers actually use. Formatting differences alone are not a ranking defect. Do not recommend bulk directory packages, a citation count target or a universal consistency score.

For structured data, first find every producer on the page: theme, plugins, custom code and tag manager. **Google guidance [G15, G16]:** Google's LocalBusiness feature needs the business name and its physical address. Review and rating markup for a local business is only for sites that capture reviews of other businesses, so review stars on a business's own pages are not eligible. Never invent or expose a private address to pass a test; record the limit instead. Structured data is not GBP verification and does not guarantee local visibility. Hand implementation and validation to a schema workflow.
